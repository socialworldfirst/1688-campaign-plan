1688 from the inside · for Southeast Asia

I1I2I3I4I5I6I7I8

Shot on the walk, as conversation with Aria. I1 and I6 also to lens in the interview corner, as reels. Brackets are 1688's to fill or confirm; an empty bracket means the line is skipped, never guessed. Lazada named as family is 1688's call.

I1 · Where Alibaba started

about 40 seconds · to lens, interview corner · also on the walk

1688 is the oldest business in Alibaba.

In 1999, eighteen people started Alibaba in an apartment in Hangzhou. The first thing they built was a marketplace for Chinese businesses to find Chinese factories. That is 1688. [1688 confirms the wording]

Taobao came later. Alipay came later. Lazada, the one you know in Malaysia, Thailand and the Philippines, is part of the same family. [1688 confirms Lazada can be named]

So when you buy on 1688, you're buying from the original, the place where your Shopee and Lazada sellers' goods start.

And when it's time to pay, you can pay direct.

I2 · Why Hangzhou

about 30 seconds · walk · arrival and entrance

Why Hangzhou? Alibaba was born here, the factories are near, and so are you.

Yiwu is an hour away by train. Half of what you see in Divisoria, Sampeng or Petaling Street starts there. [1688 confirms the Yiwu line]

From the port at Ningbo, a container reaches Port Klang, Laem Chabang or Haiphong in days, not weeks. [forwarder confirms typical times]

Southeast Asia is the closest region to these factories. That's why my team sits in this building.

I3 · Who actually buys here

about 30 seconds · walk · offices

Who buys on 1688? Shops. Restaurants. Online sellers. Factories buying from factories. Large companies buying supplies.

From Southeast Asia it's the same picture. A Shopee seller in Johor. A TikTok Shop seller in Bangkok. A garment factory in Ho Chi Minh City buying fabric. A pharmacy chain in Manila buying shelving. [1688 fills one real example per market if it can]

Most are small. Some are very large. The same platform, the same price tiers, for both.

I4 · Industrial belts

about 40 seconds · walk · showroom

China makes things in clusters. One town makes socks. One city makes lighting. One district makes phone cases. We call them industrial belts.

Your local wholesaler already knows this. The fabric in Tanah Abang comes from Shaoxing. The phone cases in Sampeng come from Shenzhen. The plastic homeware at every pasar malam in Malaysia comes from Taizhou. [1688 confirms the examples]

On 1688 you search by belt. Type the product, see the town, see the factories in that town.

That's how your wholesaler finds the source. Now you can too.

I5 · How a supplier gets on, and gets off

about 40 seconds · walk · showroom

Not anyone can sell here.

A supplier registers with a business licence, pays for a verified membership, and passes checks. A factory can add a factory verification, where a third party visits the plant. [1688 confirms the steps and the names]

Suppliers get removed. Bad ratings, disputes, fake goods: first the badge goes, then the shop. [1688 confirms]

This matters more for you than for a buyer in China, because you can't drive to the factory on a Saturday. The badge is your visit.

I6 · How 1688 changed, and why Southeast Asia first

about 50 seconds · to lens, interview corner · also on the walk

1688 has changed three times.

First a listing board. Then a trading platform, with payment, protection and logistics inside the order. [1688 confirms the years]

Now we're opening to buyers outside China, and Southeast Asia is first. [1688 confirms the first markets]

Why first? You're the closest region to our factories. Your sellers already sell Chinese goods on Shopee, Lazada and TikTok Shop. And under the ASEAN-China free trade agreement, most of those goods enter your country with low or zero duty. [trade fact, WorldFirst confirms the wording]

In 2023 Alibaba made 1688 one of its four strategic businesses. [suggested fact, 1688 confirms] Southeast Asia is part of that plan.

And when it's time to pay, you can pay direct.

I7 · The wholesale market of China, online

about 30 seconds · walk · showroom or goods-out

Every city in China has a wholesale market. 1688 is all of them, online.

Think of Chatuchak, Divisoria, Tanah Abang and Chợ Lớn in one app, at the factory price instead of the stall price.

New products appear here first, before they reach your local market. What sells on Shopee Malaysia next Raya is on 1688 now.

If you want to know what Southeast Asia will sell next season, look at 1688 this season.

I8 · Ary's own path

his own words · walk · prompted in Chinese, no sheet

Prompt: how he joined, what the Southeast Asia job is day to day, which market he visits most, the last Southeast Asian buyer he met.

Landing line one: "My job is to make a buyer in Kuala Lumpur feel like a buyer in Hangzhou."

Landing line two: "I've been to [Ary fills the cities]. The question is always the same: can I do this myself? Yes, you can."

L1 · Is 1688 only for small buyers?

about 45 seconds · to lens

No. Large companies in China buy on 1688 too. Chain stores, manufacturers, e-commerce companies, procurement teams. [1688 fills an example]

In Southeast Asia the large buyers we see are distributors, retail chains and factories. A convenience chain in Thailand, a homeware distributor in Malaysia, a garment factory in Vietnam. [1688 confirms or swaps the examples]

The same supplier shows a price for one carton and a price for one container.

If you run a chain in Bangkok or a distribution business in Kuala Lumpur, you're not too big for 1688. You're the buyer the factories want most.

And when it's time to pay, you can pay direct.

L2 · Custom manufacturing, OEM and private label

about 45 seconds · to lens

Yes. You can have products made to your design on 1688. [1688 confirms the feature name]

Search for factories, not listings. Send your drawing or your sample. Get quotes. Compare.

The private-label goods you see in Watsons, Big C or Shopee Mall every day: many are made by factories on 1688. [1688 confirms the wording, or keep it to "brands you know"]

Start with a small run, then scale with the same factory. And if your market needs halal certification, ask for it in the quote. [1688 fills: halal-certified supplier filter, if it exists]

And when it's time to pay, you can pay direct.

L3 · Inputs for Southeast Asian manufacturers

about 45 seconds · to lens

If you run a factory in Vietnam, Thailand or Malaysia, 1688 is your supply chain too.

The garment factories in Ho Chi Minh City buy fabric, zips and thread from China. The electronics assemblers in Penang and Bac Ninh buy components. The food and cosmetics makers in Bangkok buy packaging. [1688 fills: top categories Southeast Asian manufacturers buy]

Your competitors in Guangdong buy the same inputs on 1688 at the factory price. Now you can buy at that price too.

By road from Guangxi into Vietnam, inputs arrive in about two days. [forwarder confirms typical times]

And when it's time to pay, you can pay direct.

L4 · Large orders: quote, contract, deposit and balance

about 50 seconds · to lens

For a large order, you ask for a quote. [1688 confirms the RFQ feature name]

Send quantity, spec, delivery date and your port: Port Klang, Laem Chabang, Haiphong, Manila. Factories quote. You compare, negotiate, and confirm the order inside the platform. [1688 confirms]

Payment can be deposit and balance, both through 1688 World Pay, in yuan, from your WorldFirst account. [1688 and WorldFirst confirm partial payment and limits]

Ask the factory for the certificate of origin with the shipment. Under the ASEAN-China agreement it can cut your duty to zero on many goods. [trade fact, WorldFirst confirms the wording]

The order stays inside the platform, at any size.

And when it's time to pay, you can pay direct.

L5 · Twenty suppliers, one account

about 30 seconds · Ary, then Aria · same framing

Ary: To 1688, a large order from Bangkok is a normal order. Paid and tracked like any order from a buyer in China.

Aria: If you buy from twenty suppliers a month, you pay twenty suppliers from one WorldFirst account, in one batch. Fund it in ringgit, baht or dong, hold yuan, and pay when you choose. Your finance team in Kuala Lumpur sees one account and one statement. [WorldFirst confirms funding currencies per market, batch, and limits]

L6 · What a procurement team needs

about 35 seconds · to lens

Procurement teams in Southeast Asia ask us three things. Invoices for the auditor. Documents for customs. Audits for the board.

Factories on 1688 show licences and certificates on the listing. Your forwarder prepares the export documents your customs needs, including the certificate of origin. A verified factory has a third-party audit on file. [1688 confirms each line; any line not confirmed is cut]

And when it's time to pay, you can pay direct.

L7 · Second sources

about 35 seconds · to lens

Big buyers don't want one factory. They want three.

On 1688 the same product has many factories in the same belt. You qualify two or three, split the order, and switch if one is late.

For a chain in Manila or Jakarta, that's the difference between a full shelf for Christmas or Lebaran and an empty one.

That's how Chinese brands manage risk. Now it's your tool too.

And when it's time to pay, you can pay direct.

L8 · Why 1688 wants Southeast Asia's bigger businesses

about 40 seconds · to lens

Why do we want the large buyers from Southeast Asia? Because the factories want them.

A factory in Guangdong would rather sell one container to a chain in Bangkok than one carton to a hundred shops. Bigger orders, steadier orders, a better price for the buyer.

Southeast Asia is the closest region to our factories, one of the fastest-growing, and your countries already trade more with China than with anyone else. [trade fact, confirm wording]

So we built the payment with WorldFirst first, and we're building the rest.

And when it's time to pay, you can pay direct.
